## Note on Literature Review (2026-11-04)

After Stage 2b, the following questions were revised to reflect the downgrade of A2, A5, and L2 from Distinct to Reorganized:

- **RQ-B2 (Transformation Authority):** now asks how it *diverges* from Production Authority (rather than whether it exists as a distinct authority).
- **RQ-B5 (Interpretive Authority):** now asks how it operates as a structural position (rather than whether it is distinct).
- **RQ-C2 (Path Determinants):** now includes transformation as a stage that may be skipped, merged, or present — not as a distinct stage.

The questions remain as formulated; their framing reflects the literature review findings.

## 1. Purpose

The questions are formulated, not answered; the methods by which they will be examined are not specified here. The research questions are organized into four families.

| Family | Focus |
|---|---|
| A. Conceptual | The analytical categories themselves |
| B. Authority | The distribution and exercise of authority |
| C. Lifecycle | The paths and interruptions of representations |
| D. Boundary | The relation to Research I and to methodological scope |

Questions in families A–C are foundational to Research II. Questions in family D are boundary questions, classified separately.

---

## 2. Family A — Conceptual Questions

### RQ-A1

Are the five functional units (Producer, Model, Platform, Distribution Interface, Recipient) analytically distinct in actual systems, or do they routinely merge in ways that require a different analytical vocabulary?

### RQ-A2

Are the five authority types exercised by distinct entities, or do specific configurations of concentration appear systematically?

### RQ-A3

What structural conditions determine whether functions and authorities coincide or diverge?

These questions test the architecture's own categories against the systems it seeks to describe.

---

## 3. Family B — Authority Questions

- **RQ-B1.** How is Production Authority distributed across entities in AI-mediated representational systems?
- **RQ-B2.** How is Transformation Authority distributed, and under what conditions does it diverge from Production Authority?
- **RQ-B3.** How is Selection Authority distributed, and what criteria govern the selection of representations for presentation?
- **RQ-B4.** How is Distribution Authority distributed, and what conditions constrain access, reach, and retention?
- **RQ-B5.** How is Interpretive Authority distributed, and whose interpretations are presented as authoritative?
- **RQ-B6.** What patterns of authority distribution recur across different systems, and what structural factors explain these patterns?

---

## 4. Family C — Lifecycle Questions

- **RQ-C1.** Which lifecycle paths (canonical, merged, skipped, distributed, iterative) appear in practice, and with what frequency?
- **RQ-C2.** What structural factors determine which path a representation takes?
- **RQ-C3.** Where do structural interruptions occur, and what do they reveal about the exercise of authority?
- **RQ-C4.** How do re-origination cycles interact with the lifecycle's earlier stages, and what structural transformations occur across cycles?
- **RQ-C5.** How does the Recipient's interpretive authority operate on representations that have passed through different lifecycle paths?

---

## 5. Family D — Boundary Questions

### RQ-D1 (Boundary with Research I)

How does the structural distribution of authority over representations affect the recipient's epistemic standards, as examined in Research I (*Epistemic Discipline in the Age of Artificial Intelligence*, Baladi, 2026)?

**Status:** Classified as a boundary question, not a foundational question of Research II. It examines a potential relation between the two programs without assuming that Research I's findings apply to Research II, or that Research II's findings determine Research I's conclusions. The two programs remain methodologically independent; RQ-D1 concerns their possible intersection, not an integration of the two.

### RQ-D2 (Methodological Scope)

What methodological approaches are adequate for examining the questions in families A–C: analytical, empirical, or both?

**Status:** Deferred until the architecture (Conceptual, Authority, Lifecycle) is adopted. The methodology of Research II is not fixed in advance.

---

## 6. Priority and Sequencing

| Priority | Questions | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| First | RQ-A1, RQ-A2, RQ-A3 | Test the architecture's own categories. |
| Second | RQ-B1–RQ-B6 | Operationalize authority distribution. |
| Third | RQ-C1–RQ-C5 | Operationalize the lifecycle. |
| Later | RQ-D1, RQ-D2 | Boundary and methodological questions. |

This sequencing reflects the architecture's dependency structure: conceptual questions ground authority questions, authority questions ground lifecycle questions, and boundary/methodological questions follow once the foundational questions are examined.

---

## 7. What These Questions Do Not Assume

The research questions deliberately do not assume that the five functional units are always distinct, that the five authorities are always separated, that the canonical lifecycle path is normative, that interruptions are failures, that authority and function align, that Research I's findings determine Research II's questions, or that a specific methodology is required.
