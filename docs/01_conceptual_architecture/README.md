# Conceptual Architecture — v0.2 (ADOPTED)

**Status:** Adopted, version 0.2.
**Source:** Section 3 of the consolidated manuscript.
**Depends on:** Nothing prior — establishes the functional vocabulary.

---

## 1. Purpose and Non-Commitments

The Conceptual Architecture specifies the analytical units used throughout the program and the functional relations between them. It does not classify the program as theoretical, analytical, or empirical; does not specify platforms, venues, or publication formats; does not fix the methodology of future studies; and does not adopt pre-committed conclusions about who controls what. It establishes only the analytical vocabulary and the relations between its units.

---

## 2. Functional Units, Not Entities

The architecture distinguishes five functional units, defined by function rather than by institutional identity, ownership, or technical component.

| # | Functional Unit | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Producer | Originates or supplies the material from which the representation is constituted. |
| 2 | Model / Generative System | Transforms inputs into a representation. |
| 3 | Platform / Controller | Governs the conditions of production, presentation, and availability. |
| 4 | Distribution Interface | Determines how the representation reaches and appears to the recipient. |
| 5 | Recipient / Consumer | Receives the representation and engages with it. |

A single entity may perform more than one function. Two or more entities may jointly perform a single function. A function may be distributed across entities, or may be internal to a single technical system.

**Foundational assumption (negative):** Research II does not assume that each function has a distinct owner, nor that any one function subsumes the others.

This negative assumption is itself an object of investigation. The program's first task is not to assign owners to functions, but to examine the patterns of separation, merger, and distribution that actually occur.

---

## 3. Functional Descriptions

### Producer

Responsible for originating or supplying the semantic, informational, or representational material from which a representation is constituted, through direct authorship, source selection, framing, or the provision of inputs that materially shape the resulting representation. Providing an input does not by itself establish authorship of the resulting representation; this relationship remains an analytical question. The Producer is defined by the act of origination or material supply, not by ownership of the technical system.

### Model / Generative System

Performs the transformation of inputs into a representation. Its function is technical and operational. It is not by itself a Producer unless it also originates the representation, and not by itself a Controller unless it also governs the conditions of production.

### Platform / Controller

Governs the conditions under which representations are produced, presented, and made available, including policy on what may be produced, what may be displayed, ordering, ranking, filtering, availability, access, and retention. It is defined by the exercise of governance over conditions, not by ownership of the Model.

### Distribution Interface

Determines how the representation reaches the recipient and how it appears — a technical interface, a policy-driven interface (ranking, sorting, filtering, visibility rules), or an integration of both. It is not by itself a Controller unless it also exercises governance over conditions, but it may be the site where control is materially enacted.

### Recipient / Consumer

Receives the representation and engages with it through interpretation, evaluation, acceptance, rejection, further use, or further distribution. The program treats the Recipient as an active participant whose interpretive authority is itself one of the elements under examination, not as a passive endpoint.

### Role Transition and Feedback

A Recipient may also perform the Producer function within the same system or across subsequent representational cycles; reception may lead to reinterpretation, modification, recombination, or the production of a new representation. Such transitions do not collapse the Recipient and Producer functions — they indicate that functional roles may change across stages or cycles, and that a single entity may occupy different functional positions over time.

## 4. Relations Between Functions

| Relation | Description |
|---|---|
| Origination → Transformation | The Producer's output feeds the Model / Generative System as input. |
| Transformation → Presentation | The Model's output is presented through the Distribution Interface. |
| Governance over Origination | The Platform / Controller may constrain what the Producer can originate. |
| Governance over Transformation | The Platform / Controller may constrain how the Model can transform. |
| Governance over Presentation | The Platform / Controller may constrain what the Distribution Interface presents. |
| Presentation → Reception | The Distribution Interface's output reaches the Recipient. |
| Reception → Re-origination | A recipient may transform, reinterpret, or reuse a received representation to originate a new representation. |

These relations are analytical, not temporal or technical in themselves; their operational sequencing is specified by the Representation Lifecycle (Section 5).

---

## 5. Patterns of Functional Configuration

The architecture does not prescribe a fixed configuration of the five functions. It distinguishes five provisional configuration patterns.

| Pattern | Description |
|---|---|
| Concentrated Configuration | Multiple functions are performed by a single entity. |
| Partially Integrated Configuration | Two or more functions are combined within an entity, while others remain distributed. |
| Functionally Separated Configuration | Each function is performed by a distinct entity or operational component. |
| Distributed Function | A single function is jointly performed by multiple entities or components. |
| Hybrid Configuration | Different functions exhibit different degrees of concentration, separation, or distribution. |

---

## 6. Five Foundational Distinctions

1. **Production ≠ Control** — the authority to originate a representation is not the same as the authority to govern its production or presentation.
2. **Control ≠ Distribution** — the authority to govern conditions is not the same as the authority to determine the presentation interface.
3. **Distribution ≠ Reception** — the interface that presents a representation is not the same as the recipient who receives it; interpretive engagement is not determined by the interface alone.
4. **Function ≠ Owner** — no function is assumed to correspond to a specific entity; functions are analytical, owners are empirical.
5. **Governance ≠ Execution** — the authority to establish or modify conditions is distinct from the operational execution of those conditions.

---

## 7. What This Architecture Excludes

The Conceptual Architecture deliberately does not classify the program as theoretical, empirical, or methodological; specify platforms, venues, or publication formats; adopt pre-committed conclusions about the distribution of authority; assume that any single entity controls the full chain; assume the Recipient is passive, the Model a neutral transformation, the Platform external to the Model, or the Distribution Interface technical only; or assume a single direction of representational flow across cycles.

These exclusions are commitments to keep the analytical frame open until the Authority Structure and Representation Lifecycle are specified.
