## Note on Literature Review (2026-11-04)

Stage 2b of the literature review (Section 2) downgraded two authorities from **Distinct** to **Reorganized**:

- **A2 (Transformation Authority)** — the act of transformation is theorized (editing, moderation, re-ranking), but the *authority* to transform is not systematically separated from production or selection.
- **A5 (Interpretive Authority)** — interpretation is theorized as reception and framing, but not as a structural authority position.

The distinction between **act** and **authority** remains Research II's analytical contribution. The five authorities are analytically distinct; their *novelty* is integrative, not discovery-based.

---

## 1. Purpose

This document specifies the kinds of authority that may be exercised over a representation. It distinguishes five authority types, defines each, specifies its object, and identifies the structural questions that follow from its exercise. It does not assign authority to specific entities, and does not assume that any entity exercises all five authorities, nor that the five always appear together.

---

## 2. The Five Authorities

| Authority | Object | Central Question |
|---|---|---|
| Production Authority | The representation as originated | Who has the authority to originate this representation? |
| Transformation Authority | The representation as modified | Who has the authority to modify or reshape this representation? |
| Selection Authority | The representation as presented | Who has the authority to decide what is shown and what is not? |
| Distribution Authority | The representation as available | Who has the authority to determine access, reach, and retention? |
| Interpretive Authority | The representation as interpreted | Who has the authority to present a particular interpretation as the accepted one? |

**Foundational assumption (negative):** Research II does not assume that the five authorities are concentrated in a single entity, nor that they always appear in the same configuration.

---

## 3. Authority Descriptions

### Production Authority

The authority to originate a representation — to constitute the material from which it is formed — exercised through direct authorship, source selection, framing, or input provision that materially shapes the resulting representation. Providing an input does not by itself confer Production Authority over the output; this relation is an analytical question, not an assumption.

### Transformation Authority

The authority to modify or reshape a representation after origination, exercised through editing, rephrasing, re-ranking, re-contextualizing, or re-generating from source. Not the same as Production Authority: a representation may be produced by one entity and transformed, with or without authorization, by another.

### Selection Authority

The authority to decide what is shown and what is not, exercised through inclusion, exclusion, prioritization, ordering, or filtering. Operates on the set of representations available to a recipient and determines the composition of what the recipient encounters.

### Distribution Authority

The authority to determine access, reach, and retention of a representation, exercised through access control, licensing, geographic restriction, temporal availability, or retention policy. Not the same as Selection Authority: a representation may be selected for presentation but restricted in distribution, or distributed broadly without being prominently selected.

### Interpretive Authority

The authority to present a particular interpretation of a representation as the accepted one, exercised through framing, contextualization, commentary, classification, or designation of an interpretation as authoritative. Interpretive Authority is the most structurally delicate of the five: it concerns not what is produced, selected, or distributed, but what is taken to mean.

---

## 4. Authority Distribution Patterns

| Pattern | Description |
|---|---|
| Consolidated Authority | A single entity exercises all five authorities. |
| Partially Separated Authority | Some authorities are concentrated, others are distributed. |
| Fully Separated Authority | Each authority is exercised by a distinct entity. |
| Distributed Authority | A single authority is jointly exercised by multiple entities, or distributed across components. |
| Hybrid Distribution | Different authorities exhibit different degrees of concentration, separation, or distribution. |

These patterns are provisional analytical starting points, not a taxonomy of actual configurations.

---

## 5. Relations Between Authorities

The five authorities are not independent; they interact in structurally important ways. The architecture specifies ten pairwise distinctions, of which five are illustrated below; the remaining five follow the same logic.

- **Production ⊥ Transformation** — an entity may produce without authority to transform, or transform representations it did not produce.
- **Transformation ⊥ Selection** — an entity may transform without deciding whether a representation is shown, or select without modifying.
- **Selection ⊥ Distribution** — an entity may select for presentation without controlling broader distribution, or distribute widely without selecting for specific presentation.
- **Distribution ⊥ Interpretation** — an entity may distribute without framing interpretation, or frame interpretation without controlling distribution.
- **Production ⊥ Interpretation** — an entity may produce without determining interpretation, or interpret a representation it did not produce.

Each pair is analytically separable even where it is empirically joined in a specific system.

---

## 6. The Central Structural Question

Which authorities are concentrated, which are separated, and which are distributed — and what structural consequences follow from each configuration?

This question is not answered by the Authority Structure itself; it is the object of the program's subsequent analytical and empirical work. The architecture provides the vocabulary; the program examines the actual distributions.

---

## 7. Authority and Function

Authority is not the same as function. A function is what is done; an authority is the right to do it. The mapping between the five functions (Section 3.2) and the five authorities is not fixed: a function may be exercised without a corresponding authority (an unauthorized act), an authority may be held without the corresponding function being exercised (a reserved right), and the same function may be authorized by different authorities in different systems. This distinction prevents the architecture from collapsing "who does what" into "who has the right to do what."

---

## 8. Governance ≠ Execution (Reaffirmed)

The Conceptual Architecture established that governance is not execution. This holds for each authority individually: the authority to establish a rule governing Production, Transformation, Selection, or Distribution is distinct from the authority to execute it. An entity may hold authority to establish rules without holding authority to execute them; another may execute rules without holding authority to establish them.

---

## 9. What This Document Excludes

The Authority Structure deliberately does not assign the five authorities to specific entities, assume a single distribution pattern, assume that authority and function are aligned, assume authority is always exercised or always visible to the recipient, specify enforcement mechanisms, or adopt a normative stance on which distribution patterns are preferable.
