## 2.1 Introduction

Research II proposes a five-function architecture, a five-authority structure, and a six-stage representation lifecycle for analyzing how representations are constituted, controlled, and consumed in AI-mediated systems. Before any claim of conceptual originality can be made, these categories must be positioned against existing scholarship.

This section reports a three-stage literature review. Stage 1 classified each of the sixteen categories (five functions, five authorities, six lifecycle stages) against five adjacent traditions. Stage 2 proposed fifty-six primary references and verified their metadata. Stage 2b conducted targeted verification of the categories initially classified as Distinct.

The review does not claim that Research II's architecture is unprecedented. It asks, instead, how each category relates to existing work: whether it is **redundant** (already present in equivalent form), **reorganized** (present but grouped differently), or **distinct** (no clear counterpart).

## 2.2 Method

### 2.2.1 Traditions Examined

Five adjacent traditions were examined:

1. **Platform governance and content moderation** — examines selection and distribution authority in social and search platforms.
2. **Media sociology and the political economy of communication** — has long examined production and control as potentially distinct functions.
3. **Algorithmic accountability and recommender-system research** — examines ranking, filtering, and interpretive framing as distinct technical operations.
4. **Actor-network theory and infrastructure studies** — offers vocabulary for distributed, non-unitary agency across technical and organizational components.
5. **Human-AI interaction research on trust, reliance, and interpretation** — bears on the recipient's interpretive position.

### 2.2.2 Categories Examined

Sixteen categories across three types:

| Type | Count | Categories |
|---|---|---|
| Functions | 5 | Producer, Model/Generative System, Platform/Controller, Distribution Interface, Recipient/Consumer |
| Authorities | 5 | Production, Transformation, Selection, Distribution, Interpretive |
| Lifecycle Stages | 6 | Origination, Transformation, Selection, Distribution, Reception, Re-origination |

### 2.2.3 Classification Criterion

Each category was classified as:

- **Redundant** — already present in equivalent form in the literature.
- **Reorganized** — present, but grouped differently.
- **Distinct** — no clear counterpart.

### 2.2.4 Reference Base

Fifty-six primary references were proposed, distributed across the five traditions:

| Tradition | References |
|---|---|
| Platform Governance | 10 |
| Media Sociology | 12 |
| Algorithmic Accountability | 12 |
| ANT / Infrastructure | 10 |
| HAI | 12 |
| **Total** | **56** |

Metadata (author, year, title, publisher) was verified from domain knowledge. DOI confirmation was partial: four DOIs were confirmed, fifty-two remain pending manual verification.

## 2.3 Findings

### 2.3.1 Redundant Categories

Four categories are classified **Redundant** — they exist in equivalent form in the literature:

| Category | Primary Tradition |
|---|---|
| **F3** — Platform/Controller | Platform governance |
| **F4** — Distribution Interface | Platform governance |
| **A3** — Selection Authority | Platform governance |
| **L3** — Selection | Platform governance |

**Implication:** Research II should not claim originality for these categories. Their value lies in their relation to the other categories, not in their novelty.

### 2.3.2 Reorganized Categories

Eleven categories are classified **Reorganized** — they exist in adjacent traditions but are organized differently:

| Category | Primary Tradition |
|---|---|
| **F1** — Producer | Media sociology |
| **F2** — Model/Generative System | Algorithmic accountability |
| **F5** — Recipient/Consumer | HAI |
| **A1** — Production Authority | Media sociology |
| **A2** — Transformation Authority | Algorithmic accountability (partial) |
| **A4** — Distribution Authority | Platform governance |
| **A5** — Interpretive Authority | HAI (partial) |
| **L1** — Origination | Media sociology |
| **L2** — Transformation | Algorithmic accountability (partial) |
| **L4** — Distribution | Platform governance |
| **L5** — Reception | HAI |

**Implication:** Research II's contribution to these categories is **integrative** — not the discovery of new categories, but their arrangement into a coherent architecture. The five-function architecture and five-authority structure integrate categories that exist separately in different traditions.

### 2.3.3 Distinct Categories

One category is classified **Distinct** — it has no clear counterpart:

| Category | Distinct Claim |
|---|---|
| **L6** — Re-origination | The recipient's production of a new representation is not theorized as a structural stage in any tradition. |

**Implication:** L6 is Research II's strongest conceptual contribution. It completes the lifecycle loop from reception back to production, and it is not theorized in platform governance, media sociology, algorithmic accountability, ANT, or HAI.

### 2.3.4 Stage 2b Downgrades

Stage 2b reclassified three categories that Stage 1 had classified Distinct:

| Category | Stage 1 | Stage 2b | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A2** — Transformation Authority | Distinct | ⚠️ Partially equivalent | Transformation as an act is theorized (editing, moderation, re-ranking), but not as a distinct authority. |
| **A5** — Interpretive Authority | Distinct | ⚠️ Partially equivalent | Interpretation is theorized as reception and framing, but not as a structural authority position. |
| **L2** — Transformation | Distinct | ⚠️ Partially equivalent | Transformation as a technical stage is theorized, but not as a structural stage. |

**Implication:** The distinction between **act** and **authority**, and between **technical stage** and **structural stage**, is Research II's analytical contribution — not the discovery of transformation or interpretation as such.

## 2.4 Discussion

### 2.4.1 Integrative, Not Discovery-Based

The literature review supports an **integrative** claim, not a discovery-based one. Research II does not introduce new categories; it arranges existing categories into a coherent architecture. The five-function architecture, five-authority structure, and six-stage lifecycle are valuable not because their components are new, but because their **relations** are specified with precision.

### 2.4.2 The Strongest Contribution: L6 (Re-origination)

Among the sixteen categories, **L6 (Re-origination)** is the only one that remains genuinely distinct after Stage 2b. It is the point at which reception returns to production, entering the lifecycle again. No tradition theorizes this return as a structural stage.

### 2.4.3 The Analytical Distinctions

The most important contributions are not the categories themselves but the **distinctions** between them:

- **Production ≠ Control** — from media sociology, but not systematically applied to AI-mediated systems.
- **Authority ≠ Function** — from political theory, but not applied to representational systems.
- **Transformation as authority** (not just act) — from algorithmic accountability, but not separated from production or selection.
- **Interpretation as authority** (not just reception) — from HAI, but not treated as a structural position.

These distinctions are Research II's analytical contribution. They are not new concepts; they are new **separations**.

## 2.5 Limitations

### 2.5.1 Partial DOI Verification

Only four of fifty-six DOIs were confirmed. The remaining fifty-two require manual verification against Google Scholar, Scopus, or publisher websites before the manuscript cites them.

### 2.5.2 Automated Verification Is Not Sufficient

The Stage 2 verification was conducted from domain knowledge, not from live database queries. It confirms author, year, title, and publisher with high confidence, but does not confirm DOIs.

### 2.5.3 Classification Is Provisional

The classifications in this section are provisional. They may change after manual DOI verification, or after additional references are added.

### 2.5.4 Five Traditions Are Not Exhaustive

The five traditions examined are adjacent to Research II's concerns, but they are not exhaustive. Other traditions (e.g., science and technology studies more broadly, legal studies of intermediary liability, political theory of representation) may also bear on the architecture.

---

## 2.6 Conclusion

The literature review supports three conclusions:

1. **Research II's architecture is primarily integrative.** Eleven of sixteen categories are reorganized from existing traditions; four are redundant; one is distinct.

2. **L6 (Re-origination) is the strongest conceptual contribution.** It is the only category with no clear counterpart in the literature.

3. **The analytical distinctions are the program's core contribution.** Production ≠ Control, Authority ≠ Function, Transformation as authority, Interpretation as authority — these separations are not new concepts, but new arrangements.

No claim of general conceptual originality is made. The program's contribution is specified, not asserted: it is the arrangement of existing categories into an architecture precise enough to be tested against actual AI-mediated systems.
