## 1. Purpose

This document reports Stage 1 of the literature review: a categorical positioning of Research II's 16 categories (5 functions + 5 authorities + 6 lifecycle stages) against five traditions. Stage 1 does **not** claim originality; it classifies each category as **Redundant** (already present in equivalent form), **Reorganized** (present but grouped differently), or **Distinct** (no clear counterpart).

Stage 2 (reference-level verification) and Stage 3 (synthesis) remain to be conducted.

---

## 2. The Five Traditions

### 2.1 Platform Governance and Content Moderation

**Focus:** Selection and distribution authority in social and search platforms.

**Strengths:** Deeply theorizes Selection Authority (content moderation, ranking, curation) and Distribution Authority (access, visibility, removal). Distinguishes governance from execution (policy vs. enforcement).

**Weaknesses:** Rarely separates Producer from Model; treats the generative system as a black box; does not systematically distinguish Production Authority from Transformation Authority.

**Relevance to Research II:** The tradition that most directly informs F3 (Platform/Controller) and A3 (Selection Authority). Both are classified **Redundant** — Research II's contribution is not in these categories but in their relation to the others.

---

### 2.2 Media Sociology and the Political Economy of Communication

**Focus:** Production and control as potentially distinct functions.

**Strengths:** Long-standing distinction between authorship, editorial control, and distribution. Theorizes production as socially situated, not merely technical.

**Weaknesses:** Primarily concerns mass media, not AI-mediated systems. Does not theorize generative transformation as a distinct stage. Reception theory exists but is not integrated with production theory.

**Relevance to Research II:** Directly informs F1 (Producer) and the Production ≠ Control distinction. Classified **Reorganized** — the distinction exists but is not organized around five functions.

---

### 2.3 Algorithmic Accountability and Recommender-System Research

**Focus:** Ranking, filtering, and interpretive framing as distinct technical operations.

**Strengths:** Distinguishes ordering, filtering, and framing as separate operations. Examines the technical mechanisms by which selection is enacted.

**Weaknesses:** Often conflates selection with distribution (a ranked list is both selected and distributed). Rarely theorizes interpretive authority as a structural position.

**Relevance to Research II:** Informs L3 (Selection) and A3 (Selection Authority). Also informs the Selection ≠ Distribution distinction, which Research II treats as foundational but which this tradition often collapses.

---

### 2.4 Actor-Network Theory and Infrastructure Studies

**Focus:** Distributed, non-unitary agency across technical and organizational components.

**Strengths:** Theorizes agency as distributed across human and non-human actors. Provides vocabulary for non-unitary production (F1 + F2 + F3 as a single actor-network).

**Weaknesses:** Resists analytical separation of functions (which Research II requires). Does not distinguish authority from function.

**Relevance to Research II:** Informs the negative assumption that functions do not have distinct owners. Classified **Reorganized** — the distributed-agency insight exists but is not organized around five functions and five authorities.

---

### 2.5 Human-AI Interaction (Trust, Reliance, Interpretation)

**Focus:** The Recipient's interpretive position.

**Strengths:** Theorizes reception as active interpretation, not passive consumption. Examines trust, reliance, and interpretation as distinct phenomena.

**Weaknesses:** Treats the Recipient's interpretive position as a psychological or interactional phenomenon, not as a structural authority position. Does not connect to production or distribution.

**Relevance to Research II:** Directly informs F5 (Recipient/Consumer) and A5 (Interpretive Authority). Classified **Distinct** for A5 — interpretive authority as a structural position is not theorized in HAI.

## 4. Preliminary Findings

### 4.1 What Is Redundant

- **F3, F4** (Platform/Controller, Distribution Interface): Well-theorized in platform governance.
- **A3** (Selection Authority): The most theorized authority.
- **L3** (Selection): The most theorized stage.

**Implication:** Research II should not claim originality for these categories. Their value is in their **relation** to the other categories, not their novelty.

### 4.2 What Is Distinct

- **A2** (Transformation Authority): Not systematically distinguished from Production or Selection Authority in any tradition.
- **A5** (Interpretive Authority): Not theorized as a structural authority position.
- **L2** (Transformation): Undertheorized as a distinct stage.
- **L6** (Re-origination): Rarely theorized as a structural stage.

**Implication:** These are the categories where Research II's conceptual contribution is strongest. The literature review should focus subsequent stages on verifying whether they are genuinely absent or merely under-named.

### 4.3 What Is Reorganized

- **F1, F2, F5**: Exist in adjacent traditions but organized differently.
- **A1, A4**: Exist but not separated from other authorities.
- **L1, L4, L5**: Exist but not integrated into a single lifecycle.

**Implication:** Research II's contribution to these categories is **integrative** — not the discovery of new categories, but their arrangement into a coherent architecture.
