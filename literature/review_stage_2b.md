## 2. A2 — Transformation Authority

### 2.1 Distinct Claim

No tradition systematically separates the authority to **transform** a representation from the authority to **produce** or **select** it.

### 2.2 Search Results

| Tradition | Closest Concept | Equivalence |
|---|---|---|
| Platform Governance | "Content moderation" (Gillespie 2018; Roberts 2019) | ⚠️ Partial — moderation includes editing, but is not theorized as a distinct authority |
| Media Sociology | "Editorial control" (Hesmondhalgh 2019; Schudson 2003) | ⚠️ Partial — editorial control includes transformation, but is conflated with production |
| Algorithmic Accountability | "Re-ranking" / "re-weighting" (Diakopoulos 2016; Milano et al. 2020) | ⚠️ Partial — technical operations, not authority positions |
| ANT / Infrastructure | "Translation" (Callon 1986; Latour 2005) | ⚠️ Partial — translation is broader than transformation |
| HAI | "Explanation generation" (Miller 2019) | ⚠️ Partial — generation is a technical act, not an authority |

### 2.3 Final Classification

**⚠️ Partially equivalent**

### 2.4 Rationale

Transformation as an **act** is widely theorized (moderation, editing, re-ranking, translation, generation). But transformation as an **authority** — the right to transform, distinct from the right to produce or select — is not systematically theorized in any tradition.

The closest concept is **editorial control** in media sociology, which includes transformation but does not separate it from production. Research II's contribution is the **analytical separation**, not the discovery of transformation as an act.

**Recommendation:** Downgrade from **Distinct** to **Reorganized**. Update `review_matrix.csv` accordingly.

## 3. A5 — Interpretive Authority

### 3.1 Distinct Claim

No tradition theorizes interpretation as a **structural authority position** — the right to present a particular interpretation as the accepted one.

### 3.2 Search Results

| Tradition | Closest Concept | Equivalence |
|---|---|---|
| Platform Governance | "Framing" (Gillespie 2014; Bucher 2018) | ⚠️ Partial — framing is a technical act, not an authority |
| Media Sociology | "Encoding/Decoding" (Hall 1980); "Interpretive community" (Fish 1980) | ⚠️ Partial — interpretation is theorized as reception, not authority |
| Algorithmic Accountability | "Explanations" (Miller 2019; Wang et al. 2019) | ⚠️ Partial — explanations are generated, not authorized |
| ANT / Infrastructure | "Enrollment" (Callon 1986) | ⚠️ Partial — enrollment is a process, not an authority |
| HAI | "Trust in AI" (Lee & See 2004; Jacovi et al. 2021) | ⚠️ Partial — trust is psychological, not structural |

### 3.3 Final Classification

**⚠️ Partially equivalent**

### 3.4 Rationale

Interpretation is widely theorized as a **reception** phenomenon (Hall 1980; Morley 1980; Fiske 1987) and as a **technical** phenomenon (explanation generation, framing). But interpretation as an **authority** — the right to designate one interpretation as authoritative — is not theorized as a structural position.

The closest concept is **framing** in platform governance and media sociology, but framing is treated as an act, not an authority. Research II's contribution is the **structural position**, not the discovery of interpretation.

**Recommendation:** Downgrade from **Distinct** to **Reorganized**. Update `review_matrix.csv` accordingly.

The closest concept is **editing** in media sociology, but editing is conflated with production. Research II's contribution is the **structural separation** of transformation from production and selection.

## 5. L6 — Re-origination (Stage)

### 5.1 Distinct Claim

No tradition theorizes re-origination — the recipient's production of a new representation — as a **structural stage**.

### 5.2 Search Results

| Tradition | Closest Concept | Equivalence |
|---|---|---|
| Platform Governance | "User-generated content" (Gillespie 2018) | ⚠️ Partial — UGC is production, not re-origination from reception |
| Media Sociology | "Audience reception" (Morley 1980; Ang 1985) | ⚠️ Partial — reception is theorized, but not its return to production |
| Algorithmic Accountability | "Feedback loops" (Milano et al. 2020) | ⚠️ Partial — feedback is a system property, not a structural stage |
| ANT / Infrastructure | "Re-assembly" (Latour 2005) | ⚠️ Partial — re-assembly is a process, not a stage |
| HAI | "Iterative interaction" (Lee & See 2004) | ⚠️ Partial — interaction is a process, not a structural stage |

### 5.3 Final Classification

**✅ Distinct confirmed**

### 5.4 Rationale

Re-origination as a **structural stage** — the point at which reception returns to production, entering the lifecycle again — is not theorized in any tradition.

- Platform governance theorizes user-generated content, but not the **return** from reception to production.
- Media sociology theorizes audience reception (Morley, Ang, Fiske), but treats reception as an endpoint, not a return.
- Algorithmic accountability theorizes feedback loops, but as a system property, not a structural stage.
- ANT theorizes re-assembly, but as a process, not a stage.
- HAI theorizes iterative interaction, but as a psychological phenomenon, not a structural position.

**Research II's contribution:** Re-origination as a **sixth stage** of the lifecycle, completing the loop from reception back to production, is genuinely distinct.

**Recommendation:** Retain **Distinct** classification. No change to `review_matrix.csv`.

## 7. Implications for Research II

### 7.1 Weakened Originality Claim

After Stage 2b, Research II's conceptual contribution is **narrower** than Stage 1 suggested:

- **Only L6 (Re-origination)** remains genuinely distinct.
- **A2, A5, L2** are reclassified as Reorganized — they exist in adjacent traditions, but are not organized into a single architecture.

### 7.2 Strengthened Integrative Claim

The program's contribution is **integrative**, not discovery-based:

- The **five-function architecture** integrates categories that exist separately in different traditions.
- The **five-authority structure** separates authorities that are conflated in existing traditions.
- The **six-stage lifecycle** adds the Re-origination stage, which is genuinely new.

### 7.3 What This Means for Stage 3

Stage 3 (synthesis) must:
1. Acknowledge that most categories are Reorganized, not Distinct.
2. Argue for the value of **integration** rather than **novelty**.
3. Highlight L6 (Re-origination) as the strongest conceptual contribution.
