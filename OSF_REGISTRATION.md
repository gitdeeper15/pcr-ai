## Overview

### Research questions or hypotheses

This is an exploratory structural research protocol. No hypotheses are tested. The research questions are formulated, not answered.

Four families of research questions (20 total):

Family A — Conceptual (3 questions):
- RQ-A1: Are the five functional units (Producer, Model/Generative System, Platform/Controller, Distribution Interface, Recipient/Consumer) analytically distinct in actual systems, or do they routinely merge?
- RQ-A2: Are the five authority types (Production, Transformation, Selection, Distribution, Interpretive) exercised by distinct entities, or do specific configurations of concentration appear systematically?
- RQ-A3: What structural conditions determine whether functions and authorities coincide or diverge?

Family B — Authority (6 questions):
- RQ-B1: How is Production Authority distributed across entities in AI-mediated representational systems?
- RQ-B2: How is Transformation Authority distributed, and under what conditions does it diverge from Production Authority?
- RQ-B3: How is Selection Authority distributed, and what criteria govern the selection of representations for presentation?
- RQ-B4: How is Distribution Authority distributed, and what conditions constrain access, reach, and retention?
- RQ-B5: How is Interpretive Authority distributed, and whose interpretations are presented as authoritative?
- RQ-B6: What patterns of authority distribution recur across different systems, and what structural factors explain these patterns?

Family C — Lifecycle (5 questions):
- RQ-C1: Which lifecycle paths (canonical, merged, skipped, distributed, iterative) appear in practice, and with what frequency?
- RQ-C2: What structural factors determine which path a representation takes?
- RQ-C3: Where do structural interruptions occur, and what do they reveal about the exercise of authority?
- RQ-C4: How do re-origination cycles interact with the lifecycle's earlier stages?
- RQ-C5: How does the Recipient's interpretive authority operate on representations that have passed through different lifecycle paths?

Family D — Boundary (2 questions, deferred):
- RQ-D1: How does the structural distribution of authority over representations affect the recipient's epistemic standards, as examined in Research I? (Deferred until both programs have findings.)
- RQ-D2: What methodological approaches are adequate for examining the questions in families A–C? (Answered: Mixed Methods, adopted v0.2.)

Priority sequencing: Family A (first), Family B (second), Family C (third), Family D (later).

### Study design

This is a descriptive structural study using Mixed Methods, with three sequential phases:

Phase 1 — Document Analysis (primary):
- Systems: ChatGPT (OpenAI), Gemini (Google), Claude (Anthropic), Perplexity (Perplexity AI).
- Data sources: terms of service, content policies, transparency reports, model cards, privacy policies.
- Procedure: Each document is coded for (a) which functional units are mentioned, (b) which authorities are claimed, (c) which lifecycle stages are governed.
- Output: Function matrix, authority matrix, interruption log.

Phase 2 — Case Study (secondary):
- System: ChatGPT.
- Procedure: Detailed examination of lifecycle paths, re-origination cycles, and recipient interpretive authority.
- Output: Lifecycle path log, re-origination log, interpretive position note.

Phase 3 — Comparative Analysis (tertiary):
- Procedure: Pairwise comparison of function matrices, authority matrices, and lifecycle paths across the four systems.
- Output: Comparative matrices, pattern log.

No randomization. No experimental manipulation. No human subjects. All data are public documents.

Units of analysis:
- Representation (primary unit).
- Function assignment (which entity performs which function).
- Authority distribution (which entity claims which authority).
- Lifecycle path (which stages a representation passes through).

No between-subject or within-subject design. No counterbalancing.

## Sampling

### Data collection procedures

This study uses publicly available policy documents. No new data are generated. No human subjects are involved. No recruiting occurs.

Population and sampling frame:

The population is AI-mediated representational systems with publicly available policy documentation. The sampling frame is the four systems selected before registration:

1. ChatGPT (OpenAI)
2. Gemini (Google)
3. Claude (Anthropic)
4. Perplexity (Perplexity AI)

Inclusion criteria:
- Publicly available terms of service.
- Publicly available content policies or usage policies.
- Publicly available transparency reports or model cards.
- Publicly available privacy policies.
- Operational availability at the time of analysis (November 2026).

Exclusion criteria:
- Systems without public policy documentation.
- Systems that are not AI-mediated representational systems.
- Systems that are not operational at the time of analysis.

Source and location:

Documents are collected from the official websites of each system's developer:
- openai.com (ChatGPT)
- gemini.google.com / ai.google (Gemini)
- anthropic.com (Claude)
- perplexity.ai (Perplexity)

Expected duration of data collection:

Phase 1 (Document Analysis): 4–6 weeks.
Phase 2 (Case Study): 3–4 weeks.
Phase 3 (Comparative Analysis): 3–4 weeks.
Total: 10–14 weeks.

Procedure:
1. Download and archive each policy document (PDF or HTML).
2. Code each document for (a) functional units mentioned, (b) authorities claimed, (c) lifecycle stages governed.
3. Record coding decisions in ANALYSIS/coding_log.md.
4. Build function matrix, authority matrix, and interruption log.
5. Conduct case study of ChatGPT.
6. Conduct comparative analysis across all four systems.

No data will be generated before registration. The documents themselves exist publicly, but they have not been collected, coded, or analyzed for this protocol.

## Variables

### Manipulated variables

None. This is a descriptive study of publicly available policy documents. No variables are manipulated. No treatments or conditions are administered. No randomization is involved.

### Measured variables

Measured variables fall into four categories:

A. Functional units (5 variables):
- A1: Producer — entity that originates or supplies representational material. Measured as: presence/absence in policy documents; named entity (if any).
- A2: Model/Generative System — entity that transforms inputs into representations. Measured as: presence/absence; named system.
- A3: Platform/Controller — entity that governs conditions of production, presentation, and availability. Measured as: presence/absence; named entity.
- A4: Distribution Interface — entity that determines how representations reach the recipient. Measured as: presence/absence; named interface.
- A5: Recipient/Consumer — entity that receives and engages with representations. Measured as: presence/absence; named recipient.

B. Authority types (5 variables):
- B1: Production Authority — claimed authority to originate representations. Measured as: explicit claim in policy; scope of claim.
- B2: Transformation Authority — claimed authority to modify representations. Measured as: explicit claim; scope.
- B3: Selection Authority — claimed authority to decide what is shown. Measured as: explicit claim; scope.
- B4: Distribution Authority — claimed authority to determine access, reach, and retention. Measured as: explicit claim; scope.
- B5: Interpretive Authority — claimed authority to present a particular interpretation as authoritative. Measured as: explicit claim; scope.

C. Lifecycle stages (6 variables):
- C1: Origination — whether the stage is governed by policy. Measured as: present/absent; policy reference.
- C2: Transformation — whether governed. Measured as: present/absent; policy reference.
- C3: Selection — whether governed. Measured as: present/absent; policy reference.
- C4: Distribution — whether governed. Measured as: present/absent; policy reference.
- C5: Reception — whether governed. Measured as: present/absent; policy reference.
- C6: Re-origination — whether governed. Measured as: present/absent; policy reference.

D. Structural interruptions (variable list):
- D1: Interruption point — stage at which a representation is interrupted. Measured as: stage name (Origination, Transformation, Selection, Distribution, Reception).
- D2: Interruption type — abandonment, restriction, failure, or policy-based blocking. Measured as: category.
- D3: Interruption authority — which authority is exercised to interrupt. Measured as: authority type.

Coding scheme:

Each document is coded independently by the researcher. Coding decisions are recorded in ANALYSIS/coding_log.md. Where a document is ambiguous, the coding decision is recorded with a rationale.

No covariates. No subsetting variables. No exclusion variables (all documents meeting inclusion criteria are included).

### Indices

Three derived variables are constructed:

Index 1 — Function Separation Index (FSI):

Measures the degree to which the five functions are performed by distinct entities.

FSI = (number of distinct entities performing functions) / 5

Range: 0.2 (all functions by one entity) to 1.0 (each function by a distinct entity).
Interpretation: higher FSI = greater functional separation.

Index 2 — Authority Concentration Index (ACI):

Measures the degree to which the five authorities are concentrated in a single entity.

ACI = 1 - [(number of distinct entities exercising authorities) / 5]

Range: 0.0 (each authority by a distinct entity) to 0.8 (all authorities by one entity).
Interpretation: higher ACI = greater authority concentration.

Index 3 — Lifecycle Coverage Index (LCI):

Measures the degree to which the six lifecycle stages are explicitly governed by policy.

LCI = (number of stages explicitly governed) / 6

Range: 0.0 (no stage governed) to 1.0 (all six stages governed).
Interpretation: higher LCI = greater lifecycle governance.

All three indices are computed per system. No composite index across systems is computed; comparison is by direct index comparison.

## Analysis Plan

### Statistical models

No statistical models are used. This is a descriptive structural study, not a hypothesis test. No inferential statistics are planned.

Analysis proceeds through three qualitative-descriptive procedures:

Procedure 1 — Coding (Phase 1):

Each policy document is coded for presence/absence of:
- 5 functional units (A1–A5)
- 5 authority types (B1–B5)
- 6 lifecycle stages (C1–C6)
- Structural interruptions (D1–D3)

Coding is binary (present/absent) or categorical (named entity, stage name, authority type). No numerical estimation is involved.

Procedure 2 — Index construction (Phase 1):

Three indices are computed per system:
- FSI = (distinct entities performing functions) / 5
- ACI = 1 − [(distinct entities exercising authorities) / 5]
- LCI = (stages explicitly governed) / 6

These are descriptive ratios, not statistical estimates. No confidence intervals are computed.

Procedure 3 — Comparison (Phase 3):

Indices are compared across the four systems by direct numerical comparison. No significance testing is applied. Differences are described, not inferred.

No controls, no manipulation checks, no convergence criteria, no assumption tests.

### Transformations

Categorical variables are coded as follows:

Functional units (A1–A5): binary (0 = absent, 1 = present) + string (named entity).

Authority types (B1–B5): binary (0 = not claimed, 1 = claimed) + string (scope description).

Lifecycle stages (C1–C6): binary (0 = not governed, 1 = governed) + string (policy reference).

Structural interruptions (D1–D3): categorical (stage name, interruption type, authority type).

No centering, recoding, or transformation is performed. Indices are computed from binary variables as specified in the Variables section.

### Inference criteria

No inferential criteria are used. No p-values, no Bayes factors, no model fit indices.

Inference is descriptive:
- Presence/absence is reported as coded.
- Indices are reported as ratios.
- Differences across systems are described, not tested.
- No one-tailed or two-tailed tests are applicable.

Multiple comparisons are not applicable because no statistical tests are performed.

Interpretation of indices:
- FSI: 0.2 = fully concentrated, 1.0 = fully separated.
- ACI: 0.0 = fully separated, 0.8 = fully concentrated.
- LCI: 0.0 = no governance, 1.0 = full governance.

These are descriptive ranges, not inferential thresholds.

### Data inclusion and exclusion

Inclusion criteria:
- Documents must be publicly available.
- Documents must be from one of the four selected systems (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity).
- Documents must be official (from the developer's website, not third-party summaries).
- Documents must be dated within 12 months of analysis (November 2025 – November 2026).

Exclusion criteria:
- Third-party summaries, news articles, or commentary.
- Documents without a clear publication date.
- Documents that are not about policy, terms, or governance.

Outliers:
No outliers are defined, because no numerical data are collected. All qualifying documents are included.

All observations (documents) meeting inclusion criteria are used. No subsetting is planned.

### Missing data

Missing data are handled as follows:

1. If a policy document is unavailable for a system, this is recorded in the interruption log as "documentation unavailable."

2. If a document is available but does not address a particular function, authority, or stage, this is coded as "absent" (0), not as "missing." Absence of mention is treated as absence of governance.

3. If a document is ambiguous (mentions a function but not clearly), the coding decision is recorded in ANALYSIS/coding_log.md with a rationale. The default is to code as "present" if the function is explicitly named, "absent" otherwise.

4. No imputation is performed. No missing-data models are used.

5. If more than 50% of documents for a system are unavailable, that system is excluded from Phase 3 (Comparative Analysis) and this exclusion is documented.

### Other planned analysis

Exploratory analyses (secondary):

1. Cross-system pattern detection: After Phase 3, identify recurring patterns of authority distribution across the four systems. Patterns are described qualitatively, not statistically.

2. Lifecycle path frequency: In Phase 2 (Case Study of ChatGPT), record which of the six lifecycle paths (canonical, merged, skipped transformation, skipped selection, distributed origination, iterative re-origination) appear. This is descriptive.

3. Re-origination observations: In Phase 2, record any observed re-origination cycles. This is descriptive and exploratory; no inferential claim is made.

No additional statistical analyses are planned. No subgroup analyses are planned. No sensitivity analyses are planned.

## Other

### Context and additional information

Context of the research program:

Research II is part of a broader independent research effort. It is methodologically independent of Research I (Epistemic Discipline in the Age of Artificial Intelligence: A Three-Study Research Protocol, Baladi 2026, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23876902), but shares a general concern with how recipients come to accept AI-mediated outputs.

Research I examines the recipient's evolving interpretive standards across repeated interactions with a single system. Research II examines the structural distribution of production, transformation, selection, distribution, and interpretive authority over representations across the broader chain of functions. The two programs are independent; no finding from either is treated as a precondition for the other's validity.

Boundary question (RQ-D1): How the structural distribution of authority over representations affects the recipient's epistemic standards is recorded as a later-priority question and is not pursued in this registration. It would require both programs to have reached a stage at which their respective findings, rather than their architectures alone, could be meaningfully related.

What this registration does not include:

1. No empirical findings. No empirical work has been performed under this protocol. The manuscript presents an architecture and a research protocol, not findings.
2. No literature review claims of originality. A three-stage literature review has been conducted (Stage 1: categorical positioning; Stage 2: reference proposals; Stage 2b: targeted verification). It concludes that Research II's architecture is primarily integrative, not discovery-based. Only one category (L6 — Re-origination) remains genuinely distinct.
3. No integration with Research I. RQ-D1 is deferred by design.
4. No methodological commitments beyond those registered here. The methodological scope is adopted as v0.2 (Mixed Methods: Document Analysis → Case Study → Comparative Analysis).
